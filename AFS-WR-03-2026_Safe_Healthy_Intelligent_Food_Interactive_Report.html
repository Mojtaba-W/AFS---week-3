Monday · Food Safety & Quality
AFS-11-2026

AI, Sensors & Digital Traceability

Earlier risk detection across the food chain

How can intelligent monitoring help prevent unsafe food from reaching consumers?

AI, sensors, and digital traceability can support earlier risk detection across the food chain. The goal is not simply faster inspection, but prevention before unsafe food reaches consumers.

AI predictions require validation against reliable analytical and microbiological methods.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific perspective

Sensor data and traceability records can help identify patterns and potential hazards throughout production, processing, storage, and distribution.

Validation boundary

Predictive outputs are decision-support signals, not substitutes for validated analytical tests, microbiological examination, or food-safety controls.

Tuesday · Biological Food Preservation
AFS-12-2026

Protective Cultures & Bacteriocins

Beneficial microorganisms to control undesirable ones

Can beneficial microbes help protect food?

Protective cultures, particularly lactic acid bacteria, and antimicrobial compounds such as bacteriocins offer opportunities to control undesirable microorganisms.

Effectiveness depends on microbial strains, food composition, and storage conditions. Natural origin does not automatically guarantee safety or performance.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific perspective

Selected protective cultures and their antimicrobial metabolites can help control undesirable microorganisms in particular food systems.

Validation boundary

Strain identity, antimicrobial activity, food-matrix compatibility, storage conditions, and safety must be evaluated for the intended application.

Wednesday · Bio-based Films & Sustainable Packaging
AFS-13-2026

Food Residues → Functional Packaging

Turning fruit-processing by-products into packaging materials

Can food-processing residues become materials that help protect food?

Fruit-processing residues contain biopolymers and bioactive compounds suitable for developing functional packaging. Active films may help protect food, while intelligent indicators can provide information about quality changes.

Industrial feasibility, food-contact safety, and life-cycle impacts remain essential.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific perspective

Active packaging may interact with the packaged-food environment; intelligent indicators can signal selected quality changes. These functions are related but distinct.

Validation boundary

Material performance, migration and food-contact safety, manufacturing feasibility, end-of-life options, and life-cycle impacts need assessment.

Thursday · Functional Foods & Gut Microbiota
AFS-14-2026

Bioactive Compounds & Gut Microbiota

From food composition to bioaccessibility

How do food matrices influence the availability of bioactive compounds?

Polyphenols, dietary fibers, and other bioactive compounds may influence physiological and microbial processes. Food matrices and delivery technologies affect stability, release, and bioaccessibility.

Laboratory biological activity is not equivalent to demonstrated clinical benefits.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific perspective

Potential biological effects depend on compound identity, stability in the food matrix, release during digestion, and bioaccessibility.

Validation boundary

In vitro findings do not by themselves establish health outcomes in humans. Appropriate human evidence is needed before making clinical claims.

Friday · AI & the Future of Food Innovation
AFS-15-2026

AI-Driven Food Design

Predictive models, alternative proteins, and automated experimentation

Can AI accelerate the design and testing of future food products?

AI can support flavour prediction, alternative protein development, texture engineering, and generative food formulation. Automated laboratories may accelerate the design–build–test–learn cycle.

Predictive models must be supported by experimental evidence, safety assessment, and industrial validation.

Read on LinkedIn ↗

Scientific perspective

AI approaches can explore candidate formulations, predict selected product properties, and prioritize experiments. Automated laboratories can connect computational design with iterative learning.

Validation boundary

Model predictions require experimental confirmation, food-safety assessment, reproducibility, and validation at relevant production scales.
